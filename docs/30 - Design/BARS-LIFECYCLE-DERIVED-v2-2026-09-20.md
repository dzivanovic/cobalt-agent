## §3 THE DESIGN AS DERIVED
### 3.1 Partitioning (T1)
`system.bars` becomes a native `PARTITION BY RANGE (ts)` parent with **weekly** children. Bounds are **generated in code**, never typed: for each Monday, `datetime(y, m, d, 0, 0, tzinfo=ZoneInfo("America/New_York"))`, emitted as `timestamptz` literals; `TO` of week n is `FROM` of week n+1. A test asserts: contiguity over 2026–2027; no bound inside 04:00–20:00 ET of any calendar day; the week of 2026-11-01 is 169 h and the week of 2027-03-14 is 167 h. The parent carries `PRIMARY KEY (ticker, interval, ts)` and **`CHECK (interval = 'i1')`**. No `DEFAULT` partition. Width is revisable FORWARD only (a later period may be daily without touching a stored row); the trigger to narrow is §3.8's row signal, never a ticker count. Partition width bounds rows per partition, **not** total proof work: every writable or invalidated partition is read.

### 3.2 Partition creation, and the 04:00 poller with no partition (T2)
- **Created by migration**, ≥26 weeks ahead (PROPOSED tunable). `cobalt bars partitions ensure --horizon N` is idempotent, runs through the migration harness's connection as a named deploy step or by an operator; it is never a resident or a cron. `placement.py` gains a pattern rule admitting partition children, `bars_digest` and `bars_legacy` on the system side (today it names `bars` only, `:35`), and `_probe_all` registers them.
- **Heartbeat probe:** contiguous coverage of `[now − 15 days, now + horizon]`, by bounds, not by child count. AMBER < 8 future weeks; RED < 2 or ANY hole (PROPOSED tunables). Retention and repairs may never leave a hole younger than 15 days without the probe going RED for the duration.
- **What happens today if a partition is missing** (`radar/poller.py:96-115`): the insert raises, the poller catches `BaseException`, **scrubs the text**, records a generic per-ticker `error` and continues — ≈500 identical failures whose cause is only in the log; no crash (gemini's premise is false). **Derived behaviour:** a distinct failure class `no_partition` (the class name only, never row text). If the uncovered instant is in the CURRENT period the cycle aborts once and radar health goes RED/degraded (L9 — cards must not show Friday's last minute as live). If the hole is historical and hit by one ticker's first-sight 14-day batch, the failure stays per-ticker and names the ticker and the uncovered range. One `executemany` spanning a missing partition fails atomically (test).
- **Recovery:** `ensure`, then `cobalt archiver backfill-missing` for the named tickers/ranges inside the vendor window. Whether the next poll re-fetches a skipped minute by itself depends on `overlap_bars` (fable: self-heals, the watermark did not move; astra: not once a later watermark passes it) — **UNVERIFIED, §6 U6**; the design therefore reconciles explicitly and does not rely on self-healing.
- This is a change on the live radar's write path: smallest possible diff, ≥3 checkers (L67), its own test, and it lands with chunk 2, before any partitioned table exists in production.

### 3.3 The migration proof, with its honest cost (T3)
**Why the brief's 15.7 s fails (4 of 4):** (a) 507.3 rows/ticker/day is a blend; a liquid name carries 960, so the weekly threshold is **1,040–2,010 tickers/day**, and the brief's own table gives 44.8 s at 1,500. (b) "Only the open week is re-read" is false: `upsert` nights push the whole ≈14-day export through `upsert_bars` (`archiver/runner.py:236-253`); the poller's first sight of a ticker writes the whole export, 14 days deep, in session (`radar/poller.py:96-102`); `append` reaches today + 2 completed sessions (`archiver/reconcile.py:71`). (c) The harness connects as the docker **superuser with no `SET ROLE`** (`db.py:269-270`, `0001_schemas.sql:34-36`), and a child REVOKE does not bind a parent-routed insert — so "no role may write a closed partition" proves nothing about the one actor the proof audits.
**Derived mechanism — a sealed-partition ledger invalidated by trigger:**
- `system.bars_digest(partition, lower, upper, state, digest, row_count, schema_hash, generation, sealed_at, dirty, dirtied_at)`. `row_count` is the fold's exact count, never `reltuples`/`relpages`.
- States: **OPEN** (current week: no trigger, the 04:00 hot path is untouched; always digested in the outage) · **CLOSED-MUTABLE** (upper bound younger than 16 days: legitimately rewritten) · **SEALED** (older: no legitimate writer exists, because no export reaches it).
- A row-level trigger on every non-OPEN child sets `dirty` on its ledger row (a no-op after the first hit); triggers fire for a superuser and for parent-routed rows. A dirty SEALED partition is an **incident**; a dirty CLOSED-MUTABLE one is routine. Trigger overhead on an `upsert` night (≈2.67 M rows): **UNVERIFIED, §6 U8**.
- **In the outage** the harness digests OPEN + every dirty partition, and for the rest checks ledger membership (the `pg_inherits` child set and OIDs equal the ledger's), `relfilenode`, and that the trigger exists and is enabled. It prints per table one of **`CONTENT_VERIFIED` / `SEALED_BASELINE_TRUSTED` / `NOT_CONTENT_VERIFIED`** and never "content UNCHANGED on every table" for content it did not read.
- **Off the outage:** dirty partitions are re-digested and re-cleaned at deploy preflight (residents up) and nightly after the archiver; after EVERY deploy a post-deploy bracket re-digests the SEALED region against the ledger (one pass; ≈426 s at 72.45 M rows, residents up). **Known limit, stated:** a migration that disables the trigger inside its own transaction evades the in-outage check and is caught by that bracket the same evening, not in the outage.
- A repair of a SEALED week is a controlled **reopen → repair → re-digest → reseal**, generation + 1 — the seam option D needs (§4 item 4).
- The partition-manifest proof is **versioned separately**; MP1's PK-ordered whole-table md5 stays byte-compatible as `--proof-only --full` for historical comparison.
**Cost range (170,000 rows/s/side, ±13 %):** best case (nothing dirtied since preflight) 1,332,705 rows → **15.7 s**; `append` worst case (Tuesday deploy, 7 td) **22.0 s**; today's `upsert` mode with all three young weeks dirty (15 td) **47.0 s**. Until chunk 5 lands the proof is MP1: 106.8 s today, ≈62 s after the i1-only swap.
**MP4 (the interim) is HIS (§4 item 5).** If YES it ships as chunk 1b with BOTH guards: grok's in-outage `(relfilenode, n_tup_ins, n_tup_upd, n_tup_del)` equality per un-named table (any move forces a content digest of that table in the same run) AND fable's off-outage bracket (`bars WHERE ts < now − 16 days` digested at preflight and post-deploy, equal). If NO, MP1 stands until chunk 5.

### 3.4 How the 3,553,219 non-i1 rows leave (T4)
They never "leave" by `DELETE`: they are **excluded from the new table** and stay physically in `bars_legacy` until he says otherwise (astra's distinction, adopted).
1. **Gates before the first batch:** vendor window re-measured (U1); the 584→337 coverage question settled (U3); `df` on the postgres volume ≥ max(1.2 GiB, 1.5 × the rehearsal's measured peak incl. WAL/temp) (U4); a production-scale rehearsal on `cobalt_dev` has passed (§3.9 R).
2. **Online copy, outside any deploy window:** `interval='i1' AND ts < T0`, resumable batches, T0 = planned swap day − 16 days. From the first batch to the swap `restate`/`backfill-missing` refuse any range `< T0` (fail-loud), so the copied region has no legitimate writer.
3. **The swap deploy** (L66 residents down, L43 window, L68 gate): refuse to start while `pg_stat_activity` shows any non-migration session on `bars`; same-day preflight proves digest equality over `ts < T0` (legacy `WHERE interval='i1'` vs new); in the outage: delta copy `ts ≥ T0` (≈10 td × 266,541 = **2.67 M rows**; rate UNVERIFIED, U5), then the harness's NEW verdict **`REPLACED`** = digest equality of legacy-i1 vs new over `ts ≥ T0` (the legacy side has no `ts` index, so its read cost is U5 too); cutover locks with `lock_timeout`, abort safely; two renames; grants re-applied and asserted on parent + open child; residents restart on fresh connections; one verified poller write and one verified read before the deploy is called done. Without `REPLACED` the harness rolls the swap back by construction (`db_migrations/cli.py:689-696`: 8,834,532 → 5,281,313 rows is `CHANGED`).
4. **The outage is stated before the deploy** as delta-copy + ≈31 s digest + renames, replaced by the rehearsal's measured figure; never "sub-second".
5. **Rollback:** rename-only is valid only inside the same outage. After residents restart: **reverse delta** (rows written to the new table since the swap → `bars_legacy`, verified) then the renames. After day 15 the vendor cannot re-serve the gap and rollback no longer exists — `bars_legacy` is then an archive; it is dropped only on his word and never before day 15 has passed green.
