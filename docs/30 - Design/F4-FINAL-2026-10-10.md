## 3. The defect a refresh would expose (designed out below)
- After any write, the unit's baseline becomes the MERGED body, his lines included (`writer.py:774-795` `unit_after = merged_body`; `writer.py:800-802`).
- The next merge takes that baseline as `base` (`writer.py:886-890`). A line of his that sits in the baseline now reads base == human; if Cobalt's proposal differs, Cobalt wins (`merge.py:15`).
- So a naive refresh keeps his edit on the first refresh and erases it on the second: an edited SPY cell, or a line he inserted inside a Cobalt unit. Today this does not fire, because a healthy slot is written once (FAILED slots retry, §2 [F-16]). [F-16] **Repeated proposals can erase absorbed human edits unless ownership is retained.** So the Charter test fails on the 2nd refresh unless F4 decides ownership itself (§4.4).
