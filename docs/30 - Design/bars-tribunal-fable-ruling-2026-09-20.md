**The arithmetic HOLDS. Two of its premises do not, and the second one is decisive.**

1. **507.3 rows/ticker/day is a blend, not a ceiling.** The brief's own §2.6f shows a liquid name carries the full grid: **960 rows/day**. At 960 the same line reads 1,020,000 ÷ 960 = **1,062 tickers/day**, and at the low edge of the throughput band (166,000 rows/s: 30 × 166,000 ÷ 5 ÷ 960) **1,037**. "≈2,000 tickers/day" is the best case; the honest statement is **1,040–2,010 depending on how liquid the added names are**. The 30 s/side budget itself is assumed by the brief, not ruled by anyone.
2. **"Only the OPEN week is re-read" is false against the writers in the tree.** Three writers legitimately write OLDER weeks: (a) the live nightly mode is `upsert` — "the whole export through `upsert_bars`" (`archiver/runner.py:236-253`), i.e. ≈14 calendar days of i1 for 210 tickers, every night; (b) the poller's first sight of a ticker has `watermark = None → threshold = None` and upserts the **whole export** (`radar/poller.py:96-102`) — 14 days deep, during the live session, for every new pool name; (c) append mode's steady-state range is today + `STEADY_STATE_SESSIONS = 2` completed sessions (`archiver/reconcile.py:71`), so Monday's and Tuesday's nights write last week; and `backfill-missing` / `restate` reach the full window. So the rows the in-outage proof must treat as mutable are: **upsert mode → up to 3 weekly partitions = 15 td × 266,541 = 3,998,115 rows → 23.5 s/side, 47.0 s both** (headroom 5,100,000 ÷ 15 ÷ 507.3 = **670 tickers/day, 1.3×, not 4×**); **append mode → worst case a Tuesday-evening deploy, 7 td = 1,865,787 rows → 22.0 s both**. 15.7 s is reached only under the T3 amendment below.

**Does this change T1?** No — the mutable horizon is set by the vendor window and the write policy, not by partition width (daily partitions under upsert mode still read ≈11 td = 2.93 M rows = 34.5 s). Weekly stands. What changes is T3 (how "closed" is proved) and the headline number.

## T1 — partitioning (P3 weekly RANGE(ts), ET-aligned) — **AMEND**
P3 is the right shape: PK already contains `ts`, no FK/trigger/view, `i1_bars` and `bars_in_range` prune, replay's 15-day scan drops from the whole table to ≤3 partitions. Objection 1: the 15.7 s / "4× headroom" claim rests on a closed-ness the writers break (above). Objection 2, smaller than the brief makes it and still worth a test: for WEEKLY Monday bounds a wrong offset or even a UTC-midnight bound lands in Sunday evening / Monday 00:00–01:00 ET, where nothing trades — harmless. It bites the day the width narrows to DAILY, which is this design's own answer to growth: a UTC-midnight daily bound is 19:00 ET in winter, so AAPL's 2026-11-03 19:00–19:59 ET after-hours bars land in the 11-04 partition and meet a different retention and proof fate than the session they belong to.
**Replacement wording:** "P3 — weekly RANGE(`ts`), bounds GENERATED in code from `datetime(y, m, d, tzinfo=America/New_York)` for each Monday 00:00 ET, never typed; a test asserts every `TO` equals the next `FROM`, that no bound falls inside 04:00–20:00 ET of any calendar day 2026-2027, and that the week of 2026-11-01 is 169 hours long. The design states the proof cost as a RANGE — 15.7 s only under T3-as-amended, 22.0 s append-mode worst case, 47.0 s under today's upsert mode — and the width threshold as 1,040–2,010 tickers/day."

## T2 — future/unexpected timestamps (F2, no `DEFAULT`) — **AMEND**
F2 is right: `DEFAULT` is the one silent path. What actually happens at 04:00 on a minute with no partition: `upsert_bars` raises, `poller.py:106-115` catches `BaseException`, **scrubs the text** and records a generic `error` `PollFailure` per ticker — 500 identical failures whose cause is only in the log. Loud in count, mute in cause. It is also SELF-HEALING (watermark does not move, the next good poll re-upserts from the 14-day export), which strengthens F2. Two gaps: (i) coverage must reach BACKWARDS too — a first-sight write is one `executemany` spanning 14 days, so ONE missing or DETACHed old week fails the whole batch: a new mover first seen Monday 04:05 gets no bars and no card while its export still overlaps a detached week; (ii) a runtime DDL job is a new load-bearing resident outside the one migration harness (ADR-0008 D1) and outside `placement.py`.
**Replacement wording:** "F2 — no `DEFAULT`. Partitions are created BY MIGRATION, ≥26 weeks ahead; extending the horizon is another migration, never a runtime job. Heartbeat probe: contiguous coverage of `[now − 15 days, now + horizon]`; AMBER < 8 future weeks, RED < 2 or any hole. Retention may never detach a week younger than 15 days. The poller's failure record names the exception CLASS (`no partition`) un-scrubbed."

## T3 — the migration proof (MP4 now, MP2 later, MP1 as `--full`) — **AMEND**
Against `db_migrations/cli.py`: the harness runs as the docker **superuser** with no `SET ROLE` (`db.py:269-270`; `0001_schemas.sql:34-36` "a superuser bypasses every grant check"). So MP2's "a row-level change is impossible because no role may write" is false for the one actor the proof audits — the migration itself. Failing scenario: migration 0014 carries a stray `UPDATE system.bars SET close = round(close, 2)`; on closed weeks `relfilenode` is unchanged, row count unchanged, `relpages` unchanged (or HOT) → MP2 prints OK on 70 M corrupted prices; ATR and structural stops on Monday's cards are computed from them. MP4 has the same hole for every migration that does not name `bars`. Separately, the REVOKE breaks the three legitimate deep writers (§2.7 above): first Monday night in upsert mode → `permission denied` on last week → all 210 targets FAILED; every new pool name → permanent `error`, no bars, no card. And "row count" is unspecified: `reltuples` moves with ANALYZE → a false `CHANGED` → `cli.py:693` rolls back a good deploy with residents down.
**Replacement wording:** "MP2 — a closed partition is digested once into `system.bars_digest`. It stays WRITABLE; a row-level trigger on CLOSED partitions only (none on the open one — the 04:00 hot path is untouched) marks its ledger row `dirty`; triggers fire for a superuser. In the outage the harness re-digests the open partition + every dirty one and checks `relfilenode` on the rest; `reltuples`/`relpages` are never compared. Dirty partitions are re-digested and re-cleaned OFF-outage (deploy preflight with residents up, and nightly after the archiver). MP4 ships only together with an off-outage BRACKET: `--proof-only` digest of `bars WHERE ts < now − 16 days` at preflight and again post-deploy must be equal — the full content proof moves out of the outage, it is never dropped. MP1 stays as `--full`." Trigger overhead: `UNVERIFIED — cobalt_dev benchmark of a 5,000-row first-sight upsert into a triggered partition.`
