## 4. V3-2 — archive progress (desk resolution, §16 row V3-2)
- `BarStore.watermark()` stays for the poller and for reporting. It is NOT the archiver's progress: the poller advances `max(ts)` past bars that appear later (the desk's refuted V2-2 claim, `cto-2026-09-19.md` §13).
- **`archived_through` = the `ts` (bar OPEN, UTC) of the newest COMPLETE bar of an ACCEPTED export — a BAR timestamp, never a clock time, never a bar-close time, never `fetch_started_at`.** Monotonic across accepted runs (`GREATEST`); it never moves on a failed, withheld, empty or regressed target and never past that export's newest eligible bar. It records that the available export was processed through that key — not that the vendor supplied every bar.
- **ONE transaction on ONE connection per target** holds: the stored-range read, the inserts, the progress write, the incident writes and the accepted outcome. The store's append method and the progress/incident writers TAKE that connection. A crash between any two statements commits none of them. A failed or withheld target's transaction is rolled back; its failure evidence (the incident) is then persisted in a SECOND small transaction (Astra, V3-2: "Persist failure evidence separately after rollback").
- **Candidate range** of a target = every eligible bar (V2-3) of the download that lies in the UNION of (a) `ts > archived_through` and (b) the two most recent COMPLETED trading sessions at fetch start plus the current day — sessions being America/New_York calendar DATES that carry bars in the export (no 24-hour arithmetic; a weekend or holiday simply has no date in the export; DST-aware).
- **No progress row → BOOTSTRAP**: every eligible key of the available export is reconciled once (compare + DO NOTHING), regardless of rows the poller already stored; the report says `bootstrap`, states the bounds, and states that history before those bounds is UNASSESSED. An export with no eligible complete bar does not establish progress; the next non-empty night still bootstraps.
- A progress row is NEVER deleted — not when a ticker leaves the trader's list. A returning target meets its old `archived_through`, which is what makes the dropped-and-re-added sequence open a `gap` incident (round-2 sequence #8, SURVIVES).

## 5. V3-4 — MODE ISOLATION and the SHADOW COMPARE (desk resolution, §16 row V3-4)
- Config key `archiver.write_mode: upsert | append` (§10). The build DEPLOYS as `upsert`.
- **Dispatch on `write_mode` happens BEFORE any append-specific step** (Astra's rule). In `upsert` the WRITE PATH is today's code byte for byte: the whole export to `upsert_bars`, no completeness filter, no range cut, no comparison gate, no withholding, no new failure rule, NO `archive_progress` write, NO incident write, the same submitted-row count, the same run-report row. `_run_targets(…, mode=…)` keeps meaning the report scope; the write mode is a different name (`write_mode`) everywhere.
- COMPLETE LIST of what a night in `upsert` mode does that today's night does not — pinned by a test that fails if the list grows: (1) the run-level advisory lock of §9; (2) the SHADOW COMPARE below (reads only); (3) one added key `shadow` in `job.result` and one artifact file per night. Nothing else.
- **SHADOW COMPARE** — replaces v3's post-deploy `audit` as the evidence for the switch (three houses: an audit AFTER the 20:30 overlay reads zeros by construction). Inside the nightly run, per target, AFTER the fetch and BEFORE that target's `upsert_bars`: one read of the stored rows over the export's range, then the four-way key comparison of V2-4 computed TWICE — over the BOOTSTRAP scope (the whole export) and over the STEADY-STATE scope (range (b) of §4) — recording what `append` WOULD have done: `equal`, `differing` (with the key's timestamp, which of O/H/L/C/V, stored and vendor values after normalisation to `NUMERIC(14,4)` / integer volume), `late`, `new`, `stored_only`, `would_withhold` (yes/no), `export_oldest`, `export_newest`, fetch row count, poller member yes/no, and the label `pre-write`.
  - It is READ-ONLY on the database: no bar, progress, incident or config is written.
  - It can NEVER fail or slow-fail a target: its own `try/except Exception` (logged once per target, counted `shadow_errors`), its read under a `statement_timeout` (§10), and the target's `upsert_bars` runs whatever the shadow did. The 1.2 s pacing is unchanged.
  - Output: one JSON-lines artifact per night, `data/archiver-shadow/<YYYY-MM-DD>.jsonl` (`data/` is gitignored, `.gitignore:6`; retention §10), plus the aggregates in `job.result["shadow"]`: targets compared / errored, would-withhold count by interval and by poller vs archiver-only, volume-only vs any-OHLC, late and new counts. `cobalt archiver shadow-report [--nights N]` (read-only, reads the artifacts) prints the per-night table and, across nights, which differing keys PERSISTED / VANISHED / APPEARED — the retained baseline Astra and Grok require, because refreshed storage alone cannot show revision frequency.
- In `append` mode the comparison is the real gate (§6) and the shadow artifact is not written.
- NOT TAKEN, again, and said plainly: the poller honouring a quarantine (Astra alone in all three rounds). KNOWN LIMIT → §12.

## 6. The comparison and withholding in `append` mode (V2-4 as amended by v3; nobody objected to withholding itself)
One read of the stored rows of the candidate range per target, inside the target's transaction. Per key: in both and EQUAL after normalisation → nothing; only in the download → offered with DO NOTHING; only in storage → `stored_only`, flagged, NOTHING deleted; in both with ANY OHLCV difference → the target's outcome is `restated`: NO insert for that target tonight, stored rows untouched, progress unchanged, a `restated` incident, the target counts FAILED. Never an automatic repair, by constant factor or otherwise. Remedy = `cobalt archiver restate <ticker> [<interval>]`: PREVIEW first (affected range, the differences, what the vendor window covers, what stored history lies outside it and stays unresolved); execution only with `--apply`, inside a quiet window (§8), audit trail on the incident row.

## 7. V3-5 — gaps, empty exports, regression (desk resolution, §16 row V3-5) and V3-6 — counters (row V3-6)
- **Compare BAR timestamps only.** `export_newest` / `export_oldest` = the newest / oldest ELIGIBLE complete bar of the export (raw bounds are kept as diagnostics). REGRESSION = `export_newest < archived_through`, strictly → a `regression` incident with the observed bounds, target FAILED, progress unchanged. EQUAL is fine (an illiquid name whose newest bar has not moved succeeds with zero inserts). A half-day's newest bar is newer than last night's — fine. ONLY an ACCEPTED export updates the baseline; a failed night's observation is never compared against. A future-dated row is `incomplete` under V2-3 and is excluded, so it cannot poison anything. Passing this check does not prove the export complete — the report never claims so.
- **GAP** = progress exists and `export_oldest > archived_through` → a `gap` incident for `(archived_through, export_oldest)` is persisted BEFORE progress advances, with the export bounds and the session dates involved, worded "unavailable from the tested export interface", suspected-unavailable distinguished from proven-missing; the usable range is appended (unless withheld); the target is DEGRADED. A newer poller maximum can neither prevent nor erase it. On a bootstrap there is no former boundary: the report states the unassessed earlier history instead of inventing one.
- **EMPTY** export → already a `CollectorError` today (`collector.py:133`, `:143`); in `append` mode additionally an `empty_export` incident; an export with rows but NO eligible complete bar → FAILED, `empty_export`, progress unchanged.
- FAILED target = `restated`, `regression`, `empty_export`, any exception. DEGRADED = `gap` with its usable range inserted, or `stored_only` keys seen. `inserted = 0` because everything was already stored = SUCCESS. Any FAILED or DEGRADED target → the nightly job's result is not healthy (V2-9).
- **Counting identities** (every night's report reconciles, L57): `fetched = invalid + duplicate_input_keys + incomplete + below_range + candidates` · `candidates = already_stored_equal + differing + incoming_only` · `incoming_only = new + late`, where `late` = key ≤ the PREVIOUS `archived_through` (on a bootstrap `late = 0`) · target WITHHELD → `withheld = incoming_only`, `inserted = 0`, `concurrent_conflicts = 0` · otherwise `withheld = 0` and `incoming_only = inserted + concurrent_conflicts`. `stored_only` is reported beside the identities (its keys are not candidates). `invalid` is 0 by construction while the collector fails a whole target on one bad row (`collector.py:146-170`); the counter stays so the identity survives a collector change. Also per target: `stored_rows_read`, `bootstrap (yes/no)`, `status`, `reason`, `archived_through_before/after`, `poller_watermark` (reported separately, never used), `incident_ids`. Per run: the sums, `restated_targets`, `gap_targets`, `failed_targets`, run identity. `Rows Written` = `inserted` in `append` mode. Cost figures are labelled ESTIMATES; the run MEASURES candidate rows, stored rows read, insert attempts, committed inserts and runtime.

## 8. V3-9 — OPTION A, ruled by the owner (R8): repairs only in a QUIET WINDOW; the live poller is not touched

Both commands take ONE ticker (optionally one interval) — there is no all-targets form, so a repair transaction lasts seconds.

## 9. Failure semantics and the lock (V2-9; stands)
One transaction per target (§4). The run continues after a failed target. A Postgres session-level advisory lock, ONE constant key, taken at the start of `run_full`, `run_backfill`, `restate --apply` and `backfill-missing`, in BOTH write modes; a second holder refuses loudly (`another archive/repair run holds the lock — <what it is, if known>`), exit code 2. The poller does not take it (R8). Run identity: `system.cobalt_jobs` has one row per label and no run id (§2), so `run_id` = `<label or command>@<run started_at, UTC ISO>` — a TEXT the report and the progress row share; no new run table.

## 11. The two additive tables and the promotion path
Both SYSTEM side (L32: market-data bookkeeping, nothing of one trader's choice; `placement.py` gains both; `cobalt_user` is granted nothing on them). Logical keys only — nothing about how `bars` is physically laid out (V2-8).
- **`system.archive_progress`** — `ticker TEXT`, `interval TEXT`, `archived_through TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL`, `export_oldest TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL`, `export_newest TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL` (eligible-bar bounds of the last ACCEPTED export), `raw_export_newest TIMESTAMPTZ` (diagnostic), `fetch_started_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL`, `bootstrap_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL`, `run_id TEXT NOT NULL`, `updated_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL`; `PRIMARY KEY (ticker, interval)`; `CHECK (archived_through <= export_newest)`. Written ONLY by the archiver in `append` mode, only in an accepted target's transaction, `archived_through = GREATEST(old, new)`.
- **`system.archive_incidents`** — `id BIGSERIAL PRIMARY KEY`, `kind TEXT CHECK (kind IN ('gap','restated','stored_only','empty_export','regression'))`, `ticker`, `interval`, `range_start`, `range_end`, `first_seen_at`, `last_seen_at`, `detail JSONB`, `resolved_at`, `resolved_by`, `run_id`; ONE unresolved row per `(kind, ticker, interval, range_start)` (partial unique index `WHERE resolved_at IS NULL`) — a recurring night refreshes `last_seen_at`, never duplicates. `regression` is added to v2's four kinds because V3-5 gives regression its own incident. `cobalt archiver incidents` lists the unresolved ones; the heartbeat's archiver probe (`heartbeat/probes.py:261`) is not green while any is unresolved. Resolution is explicit (`incidents resolve <id> --by <who> --note …`), audited on the row.
- **Migrations**: database-wide, `src/cobalt/db_migrations/`, numbers **`0010_archive_progress.sql`** and **`0011_archive_incidents.sql`**, each with its `.rollback.sql` (a plain `DROP TABLE` of its own table — additive, no other object touched), registered in `FORWARD` / `REVERSE` in numeric order. `0008`/`0009` belong to the unmerged `sprint-2/p4` and are NEVER used here; a gap in the tuple is legal (the registry is an explicit list, `__init__.py:40-58`; no test asserts contiguity). The branch that lands second rebases and keeps BOTH sets in numeric order. Ownership and grants follow `0006_radar_score.sql`'s pattern for a system table — the builder reads it, never invents one.
- **Promotion (L7's shape — it IS a flip from one source of truth to another):** deploy as `upsert` with the shadow on → **N shadow nights, N = PROPOSED 5 trading nights** (one full week, so a Friday→Monday boundary and the weekly vendor window roll are inside the sample) **— N is the OWNER's** → `shadow-report` numbers to him and to the tribunal record: would-withhold targets per night, field distribution, persisted/vanished/appeared, late/new counts, measured cost of the steady-state read → **his explicit ruling to switch**, which must also accept or refuse the KNOWN LIMIT of §12 in words (Astra: "Tribunal agreement counts do not settle it"). ## 15. Tests that must exist (Grok's and Astra's lists, ALL taken, merged with v2/v3's; each new test fails before its code exists)
