## How to rule
For EACH question Q1–Q8 give exactly one of:
- `HOLDS`
- `HOLDS WITH <replacement wording, pasteable verbatim, naming the draft file and the section or line it replaces>`
- `DOES NOT HOLD <file:line of what fails, a concrete failing scenario, and the wording that would make it hold>`

then at most eight lines of reasoning. A concrete scenario is a session of a named house starting cold, the exact file it opens first, the hop where it loses a fact, and what it then does wrong — or a named apply step at a clock time and what it breaks. "I would have written it differently" is not an objection. Every byte count you rest on is re-derived from the files (steps shown) or marked `UNVERIFIED — <the measurement that settles it>`. A claim about how a CLI, a harness, auto-loading of a startup file, a watcher, a tokenizer or a model BEHAVES that the files do not settle is not an argument: put it under EXPERIMENTS — an unrun claim is unproven, never a defect (L70). Prefer the simpler mechanism; if you replace one of the proposal's, price your replacement (bytes added to which read path, files, apply steps). Cite every file you open as `path:line`.
