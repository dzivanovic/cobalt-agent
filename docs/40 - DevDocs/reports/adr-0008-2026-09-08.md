# ADR-0008 — two-layer data model: user data vs system data

**Sprint close, 2026-09-08.** Branch `sprint-2/data-model`, 35 commits off
`main` at `3c09e94`. ADR: [`docs/10 - Decisions/ADR-0008-two-layer-data-model.md`](../../10%20-%20Decisions/ADR-0008-two-layer-data-model.md).

## 1. Headline

Cobalt now knows the difference between the engine and you. Everything the
system is — bars, jobs, the kill switch, the redaction counter, the email
log, the session blocks — lives in a `system` schema. Everything that is
yours — cards, their history, day modes, the vault audit trail, your
trade definitions, your per-trade numbers, your sheet dollars — lives in a
`"user"` schema, stamped with a `user_id`. Each store picks its side and
the database enforces it: a system-side connection asking for your cards
gets *permission denied*. Your 13 trade definitions left the repo and now
live only in your strategy notes, where you can edit them; the 22 notes and
69 trade notes were migrated to match, and the sheet dollars and day-mode
rules left `configs/` for a table. **The repo installs empty: no trade
name, no sheet dollar, no cheat-sheet rule is committed any more.**

- **0 — the schema keeps the name `user`.** Reason, quoted: *"user will have
  more roles and more user settings outside of being trader only."* `user` is
  a RESERVED word in PostgreSQL 16, so it is ALWAYS quoted — every migration,
  grant, `search_path`, hand query, and `psycopg.sql.Identifier` in Python.

  field names (`traders`, `trader_settings`, `trader_id`, `trade_key`).

  one fails loud. `trade_key(slug) = slug.replace('-','_')` is the one
  grammar-safe spelling for `cfg()` keys and `per_trade()` scopes.

**Two findings recorded with the ruling.**
1. `CREATE ROLE` is a SHARED-CATALOG operation. Running the migration against
   `cobalt_dev` created `cobalt_user`, `cobalt_system` and `cobalt_backup` for
   the WHOLE cluster, `cobalt_brain` included. All three are NOLOGIN and hold
   no grant in `cobalt_brain` — every privilege they have is a per-database

## 8. ESCALATE

1. **`vault restore` is section-wide.** Restoring one unit write reverts the
   whole section, dropping any sibling unit written after it. *Why it matters:
   any note with two units cannot be rolled back per-write.*
2. **`vault restore` cannot undo an `upsert_region` write.** It finds the
   section by marker and frontmatter has none. *Why: 102 of this sprint's
   vault writes are frontmatter; the snapshot is their only rollback.*
3. **`cobalt_app` non-superuser login (owed ops prompt).** *Why: until it
   exists, a connection that skips the factory keeps superuser and bypasses
   every grant — the lint test is the only thing holding that line.*
4. **`setup_trade_matrix` is def-only.** A draft has no `trade_defs` row, so
   its seeded `valid_setups` never reach the view. *Why: S2's radar has to
   decide whether it wants draft rows.*
5. **`working_timeframe: 2m` is duplicated** in every note's frontmatter and
   in `defaults.yaml`. *Why: two homes for one fact.*
6. **`status:` is derived, not stored.** The loader computes it and reports
   drift; the frontmatter copy is decoration. *Why: decide whether to keep
   writing it at all.*
7. **The stats unit is free text** (`n: insufficient data (n<30)`). *Why: it
   wants a typed row before anything computes from it.*
8. **User values inside system config** — `redact.yaml`'s host patterns,
   `notify.yaml`'s recipient, `watchlists.yaml`'s list names. *Why: they
   cannot move to the database while F18/F19 must work with the DB down.*
9. **`configs/cobalt/strategies.yaml` is keyed by free-text strategy NAME.**
   Read only by the DRC's reversion tag. It is user data twice over (his trade
   names, his classification) and its seed list came from the dropdown D4 just
   replaced. *Why: re-keying it to slugs and moving it user-side is a
   redesign, not a rename.*
10. **The `puppy-dog` strategy note** does not exist; 1 trade note is left
    blank waiting for it. *Why: ruled as "a separate def later".*
11. **`tests/test_finviz_extractor.py` collection error** in the OLD tree.
    *Why: pre-existing on main, unowned, and it makes `pytest tests` red.*
12. **The ADR's D4 table lists the 13 slugs in a committed document.** *Why:
    it is the migration record and L31 permits trade names in docs (not
    person names) — flagged, not changed.*
13. **`configs/cobalt/taxonomy/examples/example_trade_def.md` is a `.md`
    outside the D6 tree.** *Why: it is DATA (a strategy note in the vault's
    exact shape, used as the loader fixture), not documentation — but
